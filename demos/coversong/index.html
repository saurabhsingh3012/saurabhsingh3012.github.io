Is this a cover of that song?

Two recordings can be the same song and share almost nothing on the surface: a different key, a rearranged verse and chorus, tape hiss or crowd noise underneath. Compare the raw audio and they look unrelated. The fix is to compare the chords instead, correct for key first, then let the alignment bend around whatever moved.

Everything below runs in your browser. Build a "cover" of the fixed original song by shifting its key, rearranging its sections, and adding noise, then watch what happens to the match score with and without key correction. The OTI, DTW and local-alignment math is ported from the Python in cover-song-id and checked against it line for line (see the footer).

Score after the current section edit, as a percentage of the same scorer's score on an un-edited cover (same key, same noise). DTW must pay for every frame it is dragged through, including the repeated or dropped section, so it degrades as edits pile up. Local alignment can reset and pick up the best surviving chain, so a clean repeat or drop barely moves it.

The verdict below calls anything above 0.15, half of local alignment's own ceiling, a match. That cutoff is this demo's invention, chosen so the toy examples above sort cleanly, not a number from the paper. The real project doesn't classify pairs at all; it ranks candidates by score and reports mean average precision, the way an actual cover-song search engine would.

Ported from and checked against saurabhsingh3012/cover-song-id: the Optimal Transposition Index, DTW and Smith-Waterman-style local alignment recurrences here are the same formulas as invariance.py and similarity.py, run in Python on a fixed pair of chroma sequences and again in this page's JavaScript, with matching output. That repo's real result, MAP 0.396 for OTI plus local alignment on the Da-TACOS cover-song benchmark, was measured on real recordings with real CREMA-PCP features, not on the toy chord progressions this page synthesises directly in the chroma domain for a fast, self-contained demo. This page shows why the method works; the repo holds the benchmark number.
