Acoustic fingerprinting, live

This is the guts of Shazam, built from scratch and running in your browser. It turns a track into a sparse constellation of spectrogram peaks, hashes pairs of those peaks into keys, then identifies a short excerpt by voting on time alignment. Pick a track, bury a clip of it under noise, and watch it still come back with the right answer.

Everything runs locally. Nothing is uploaded. The exact same pipeline, parameters and all, is the Python project audio-fingerprint, ported here bin for bin.

The excerpt, fingerprinted

Spectrogram of the noisy excerpt (dark is quiet, warm is loud). The dots are the peaks the picker kept: local maxima that stand above the local noise floor, spread across the spectrum by a per-band quota so a bass-heavy passage cannot hog them all. Amplitude is thrown away after this. Only where the peaks sit gets hashed, which is why noise barely dents it.

The vote

Every shared hash proposes a time offset between the excerpt and a library track. For the real match, thousands of hashes agree on one offset and stack into a single spike. For everything else the offsets scatter. The tallest spike is the score.

Pipeline: 11025 Hz mono, 1024-point STFT (Hann, 75% overlap), magnitudes in dB. Peaks are local maxima over an 11×9 bin neighbourhood that clear a local mean-plus-sigma threshold, thinned to about 30 peaks per second by a quota over six geometric frequency bands. Each anchor peak is paired with up to eight later peaks into (f1, f2, Δt) hash keys; identification is a time-offset histogram vote. On 48 real Creative-Commons tracks the Python version holds top-1 to 0 dB SNR. Source and full write-up in saurabhsingh3012/audio-fingerprint.
