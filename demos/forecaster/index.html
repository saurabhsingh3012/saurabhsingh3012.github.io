Tune a Holt-Winters forecaster until it beats naive

This runs the additive Holt-Winters recursion and the MASE metric from crate-economics, ported into JavaScript. Move the sliders below and the in-sample fit, the forecast, and the error score all update immediately.

Everything on this page runs in your browser; nothing is uploaded anywhere. The recursion, the seasonal initialization, and the MASE formula are copied from holt_winters.py and metrics.py in the repo, not rewritten from a textbook description. See the verification note in the footer.

Data: this is not a real vinyl price history. It is release R0003 from the repo's market simulator, a synthetic first pressing the simulator names "Quiet Union, Paper Cities," generated with the simulator's default seed and settings and then averaged from its native weekly cadence into 60 monthly points for a cleaner chart. Every wiggle you see, including the repress crash that lands inside the held-out year below, came out of the simulator's own AR(1) drift, seasonal demand curve, and shock model, not out of a hand-drawn series.

Math ported from saurabhsingh3012/crate-economics: the Holt-Winters recursion in holt_winters.py and the MASE definition in metrics.py. Checked against the Python source on two fixed series, the 48-point training window used above and a separate 36-point synthetic series with known parameters, run through the repo's own _run and mase functions. Fitted values, forecasts, and MASE agreed to floating-point precision in both cases (about 1e-15, ordinary rounding noise, not a discrepancy). Only alpha, beta, gamma, and season length are exposed as controls here; the repo's damping parameter and its L-BFGS-B optimizer are not part of this demo, so treat the sliders as a way to feel out the recursion rather than a search for the repo's fitted values. This single-series monthly measurement also is not directly comparable to the README's panel-level weekly MASE of about 0.79 across 40 releases: different cadence, different season length, and one series instead of a panel average.
